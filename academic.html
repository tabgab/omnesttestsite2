Academic Use

OMNEST™ is freely available for our non-commercial users under the Academic Public License. This license provides non-commercial users of OMNeT++ with rights that are similar to the well-known GNU General Public License, yet it makes it feasible for OMNeT++ authors to financially support the development by selling commercial licenses.

Several universities have chosen to employ OMNEST™/OMNeT++ in teaching as well as research, and several research groups have chosen to share their simulation models with the User Community under some open-source license. Please browse www.omnetpp.org to learn how others use OMNEST™/OMNeT++ in teaching and research, to download simulation models and to meet the User Community. Many of the simulation models are developed by or with active participation of Omnest Global Inc. people.

We would like you to understand that OMNEST™/OMNeT++ is not just a commercial product which was graciuosly made available for free for academic use. If you are an academic and you start to use OMNEST™/OMNeT++, then the work you invest is safe. The unique Academic Public License guarantees that you'll enjoy GPL-like rights as long as you stay in the non-commercial world, in any event -- even if Omnest Inc. disappears, falls to takeover or changes business strategy. You will be able to copy, modify and redistribute copies of the software as long as you stay non-commercial.

Q: Do I qualify as a non-commercial user?

A: If you are a student or university lecturer and OMNEST™/OMNeT++ is used in teaching, then it qualifies as non-commercial activity. If you are doing research with OMNEST™/OMNeT++ (whether at a university, research institute or company), then you have to ask yourself whether the results will be publicly available (i.e. published in a journal or presented at a public conference), or will be used in the creation of a commercial product. We reserve the right to make final judgment on this issue in any specific case.
